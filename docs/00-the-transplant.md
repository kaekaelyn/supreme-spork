# 00 — The Transplant

The pillar that governs every other document: **the player character is a 21st-century person.** Not a caveman, not a blank slate, not an archetype. Someone from now, with a now-shaped mind, standing naked in a conifer forest at 5 °C.

---

## 1. Knowledge without skill

This is the design's central engine, and it resolves several problems at once.

**You know things.** You know that water should be boiled and wounds should be cleaned. You know that fire needs dry tinder and airflow. You know what a bow is, what a lever is, what a kiln is. You know that fat and ash make soap, that charcoal burns hotter than wood, that you can select the tamest animals and breed them. You know a year has seasons and that you should be storing food before winter.

**Your hands know nothing.** You have never knapped stone. You have never made cordage. You have never started a fire without a lighter. You have never tanned a hide, thrown a spear, or fired a pot.

The game therefore **never needs a tutorial**, and its refusal to explain anything is not an affectation — it is the premise. You are not missing information. You are missing *practice*, and practice is the only thing the game hands out.

Every crafting system should be designed against this test: **does failure feel like "I don't know what to do" or like "I know what to do and I can't do it yet"?** The second is the game. The first is a bug.

### Corollaries

- **Your body is modern.** Unconditioned, soft-footed, and terrible at everything physical. Conditioning is a slow, real gain — and under the [1:1 clock](02-survival-systems.md#6-time--the-11-world-clock), your character genuinely gets fitter over real months. Early blisters and injuries from unaccustomed work are correct.
- **Your modern knowledge can be wrong here.** The best example: **you know a year is 365 days. It isn't.** The Cretaceous day is ~23.4 hours, so the year is about 374 days long. A player who builds a calendar on the assumption of 365 will drift, miss the solstice, and misjudge winter. The only fix is to *measure it yourself*. Modern knowledge as an active trap is the single best expression of this pillar in the game, and it should not be hinted at anywhere.
- **You are literate, numerate, and can draw.** This is enormous, and it drives the [record system](05-society-and-multiplayer.md#3-writing-two-layers). You can write real sentences and sketch real diagrams. A Neolithic person could not have left the record you can leave.
- **You have no immunity to nothing in particular.** Cretaceous pathogens are not adapted to primates, so novel plague is not the threat. **Environmental wound infection** is, and so are parasites, which do not care what you are.
- **You do not know what any of this is.** There is no taxonomy, no naming, no field guide. See [§3](#3-what-the-player-is-never-told).

## 2. The tech ceiling

**The ceiling is what a determined modern person could actually achieve from nothing** — not what they can name. This is a much sharper constraint than "stop before metal," and it is the right one.

**Reachable**, because knowledge plus available materials genuinely gets you there:

Stone tools and systematic knapping · cordage from bark bast and sinew · fire by friction and by ember-carry · hafting with conifer pitch · hide working and brain tanning · sewing with bone and quill needles · **down insulation and layered clothing** · basketry from bark and split root · fired ceramics, then kilns · **lime from burnt shell and lacustrine carbonate** → plaster and mortar · charcoal · **soap from rendered fat and wood-ash lye** · atlatl, then bow · nets, weirs, traps, deadfalls · timber joinery, post-and-beam, pit houses, sod and turf construction · tuff excavation · levers, wedges, ramps, cordage mechanics, a windlass · **selective breeding** · food preservation by drying, smoking, freezing, and fat sealing · **writing, measurement, and a self-derived calendar**

**Not reachable, and the game says so by simply not containing it:**

**Metal of any kind.** A modern person knows smelting exists. Finding workable ore alone, in this terrain, and building and running a furnace economy is not something one person or one small settlement achieves — and pretending otherwise would be the exact "reinventing that wheel" fantasy this game refuses. There is no ore prospecting system, no smelting, no forge. Also absent: the wheel as transport (no draught animals, no roads), glass, textiles from plant fibre (there is no flax, no hemp, no cotton — nothing to spin), and anything requiring a supply chain.

The endgame is therefore **not a better tool.** It is a settlement that has learned to read its valley, survived enough winters to have a memory, and written down enough that its knowledge outlives any single person.

## 3. What the player is never told

Zero diegetic information. Not softened, not partial.

- **No names.** Nothing has a species name, a common name, or a label. Not in a UI, not in a codex, not on a corpse. The small ginger animal with the striped tail is whatever you decide to call it.
- **No codex, no bestiary, no field guide, no journal-that-fills-itself.** The only record is [the one you write](05-society-and-multiplayer.md#3-writing-two-layers).
- **No date, no location, no "125 million years ago" card, no framing device.** The premise is never addressed. You might reasonably conclude this is another planet, and the game will never correct you.
- **No recipes, no tech tree, no unlock notifications, no quest markers, no tooltips.**
- **No needs bars** by default. The body reports itself: shivering, fogged breath, clumsy hands, narrowed vision, involuntary noises. An optional numeric overlay exists in accessibility settings only.

### Where the science goes instead

The scientific rigour is a constraint on **the world**, never a thing the world narrates. The educational and credibility value lives **outside the fiction**:

- The [open paleobiota dataset](../data/) — public, versioned, citable, correctable by working scientists.
- [SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md) and the design documents, published.
- An out-of-game companion (website or main-menu appendix, clearly outside the fiction) for anyone who wants to know what they were looking at.

This is a *better* outcome than an in-game codex. It keeps the game pure and puts the science somewhere a paleontologist can actually engage with it.

## 4. Where do people come from?

The world produces people. It does not say why.

You woke up naked and disoriented with no explanation, and so does everyone else. New arrivals — a new player joining, or a player returning after death — **wake somewhere in the world, naked, alone, exactly as you did**, with no relationship to where they were before.

This is the answer to reproduction, to settlement growth, and to respawn, and it is a single mechanic that costs nothing to explain because it is never explained. It also generates culture on its own: a settlement that has been going a while will eventually encounter a naked, confused stranger at its edge, and will have to decide what it does about that. Take them in? Test them? Turn them away? The game has no opinion. Whatever the settlement does the second time will be because of what happened the first time — and that is a tradition, invented at the table, about the only unexplained thing in the world.
