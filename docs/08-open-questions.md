# 08 — Open Questions

Decisions that are genuinely yours. Each has my recommendation and the reasoning, so you can disagree with the reasoning rather than the conclusion.

---

### 1. Strict Yixian, or the whole Jehol Biota?

Strict Yixian gives a coherent ~1.7 Myr window but costs us *Microraptor*, *Jeholornis*, *Sapeornis*, and most of the famous birds — all Jiufotang, ~120 Ma. See the [exclusion list](01-the-science.md#7-the-exclusion-list).

**Recommendation: strict.** The premise of this game is rigour, and "we smeared 11 million years together because *Microraptor* is cool" is exactly the compromise that would undermine it. Ship Jiufotang later as an explicitly-labelled data pack — same basin, five million years on, visibly different fauna. That's a compelling piece of content *and* a demonstration that the strictness was real.

---

### 2. Does the tech tree reach metal?

**Recommendation: no, not at launch.** Bog iron is plausible; copper is a stretch. But the real argument is design, not geology: the reflexive endgame of every survival game is metal, and reaching for it here would replace this game's actual endgame — a multi-generational settlement that has learned to read its valley — with a familiar one. Cap at ceramics, composite tools, textiles, and husbandry. Offer metal as a clearly-labelled optional server module for groups that want it.

---

### 3. How long is a year?

**Recommendation: 6 real hours by default**, server-configurable 2–40, with winter at ~35% of the year. Short enough that a casual group meets winter in their first session or two; long enough that a season has room to breathe. This wants playtesting more than it wants theory.

---

### 4. Permadeath, or something softer?

The design in [05](05-society-and-multiplayer.md) has your character die permanently and you return as a new person with no practised skills — with the twist that taught and recorded knowledge survives.

**Recommendation: ship it as the default**, but make it a server setting with a softer "inheritance" option (new character retains a fraction of practised skill). The permadeath version is what makes teaching matter, but it will lose players, and a server toggle costs nothing.

---

### 5. Do NPC humans exist?

**Recommendation: absolutely not.** Every NPC human demands an explanation for why humans are here, and the premise's power comes from refusing that question entirely. Solo means alone. The Legacy feature (arriving at a previous player's ruins) is the humane answer to solo loneliness and it's a far better one.

---

### 6. Should the game ever acknowledge *when* you are?

**Recommendation: never explicitly.** No date, no "125 million years ago" title card, no scientist's voiceover. But the codex is written by an implied observer who *does* know — using real taxonomic names and real citations. That tension is delicious: the character has no idea, the player is being handed a peer-reviewed field guide. Don't resolve it. Don't explain it.

---

### 7. PvP?

**Recommendation: off by default on public servers, on for private.** The environment is a better antagonist than other players, and the co-operative pressure of a shared winter produces better stories than raiding. But don't fight the community on it — some groups will want PvP and the systems support it fine.

---

### 8. How punishing should the first hour be?

The honest version — naked, 5 °C, wind, no tools, no instructions — has a very high chance of death within 30 minutes for a new player.

**Recommendation: don't soften the systems; soften the *spawn*.** Place new characters in a defensible spot: near a fumarole (free fire), near water, in sheltered terrain, in the warm season of the first year. The world is exactly as lethal as designed; the player is just dropped somewhere survivable. That's an honest, invisible tutorial and it preserves the premise completely.

---

### 9. Which animal is on the box?

**Recommendation: *Sinosauropteryx*.** It has the best-documented colour of any animal in the formation — ginger and white banded tail, bandit mask, countershaded — so the cover art is *literally* evidence-based, which is a marketing story in itself. *Yutyrannus* is the more obviously commercial choice, and would set exactly the wrong expectation about what this game is.

---

### 10. What's it called?

*The Down Age* is my pick — it names the material culture and doubles as a phrase about being brought low. **Ashwinter** is the more commercially obvious one. **Sihetun** (the real fossil locality) is the most evocative to people who know, and meaningless to everyone else.

---

### 11. Do we open-source the paleobiota dataset?

**Recommendation: yes, permissively, and loudly.** Paleontologists will correct it for free, which is better than any consultant we can afford. It converts our single biggest credibility risk into a community asset, and it makes the "the game updates when the science updates" promise verifiable rather than a marketing claim.
